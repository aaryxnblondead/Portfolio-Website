1a:Ta54,<p><strong>The problem:</strong> Financial risk teams need to detect when the NIFTY-50 shifts from a calm regime into a crisis regime, while also seeing which companies move together during the transition.</p>
<p><strong>The constraint:</strong> The model has to run on a 16GB GPU during backtesting windows of under two seconds per step, while still capturing cross-sectional dependencies across all fifty stocks.</p>
<p><strong>What I built:</strong> Vortex-AI turns NIFTY-50 price history into log-return windows, crisis labels, and empirical adjacency matrices. A spatio-temporal baseline and a Graph Attention Network treat each company as a node, then predict both the market regime and the changing relationship map.</p>
<p>The design decision is to make the relationship graph a first-class output instead of using it only as an internal feature. That makes the model useful for analysis: the regime score says whether the market is stressed, while the adjacency prediction shows the contagion structure behind the score. The pipeline keeps preprocessing, graph construction, training, and held-out evaluation separate so each artifact can be inspected independently.</p>
<p>The pipeline preprocesses NIFTY-50 prices into log-return windows, labels each trading day as calm or crisis, and for every 60-day chunk constructs an empirical adjacency matrix — a "who-moves-with-whom" map written as a table of Pearson correlations. Those matrices and regime labels are what the models learn from.</p>
<p>The baseline encodes each asset's return history with an LSTM, reading one day at a time. The GAT instead puts all 50 companies on a network: each node's features feed through two attention layers, then a bilinear decoder scores every pair-wise edge while global mean pooling produces the regime logit.</p>
